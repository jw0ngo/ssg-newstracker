Each one will show the event, the thesis it fits, and the SSG pitch it opens.

Situations group the stories of one deal, round or process across the weeks it runs, by the company they are about (read from the headline; a known portco or target wins) and by shared words, so twenty headlines on one takeover read as one card. The phase is the furthest any report got: Reported (explores, weighs, in talks), In process (advisers hired, data room, bids, shortlist, an unaccepted offer), Agreed, Completed. The heat is the hottest story's. A card is shown when a situation has more than one story or is in process; tap it for every story.

Priority is ordered by Heat, a 0–100 score for the Singapore SSG team: APAC relevance (25 — Singapore and SE Asia first, then India and ANZ, then North Asia), live process (20), technology intensity (20), sponsor relationship (15), deal size (10) and recency (10). Tap the flame number for the breakdown. An item is on the list when it is relevant: an APAC item whenever it carries an event or a focus area; a global item only when it is live or signed and in a focus area, or a fund close, a new platform or a senior hire. The rest is kept behind the link at the bottom; the page carries the last 90 days and the archive in the repo keeps everything. Event, thesis and APAC are read from the headline by keyword; the matched words sit under each tag. A portfolio company buying something is an add-on and is listed here with the buyer's name, whether or not the headline names the firm. A story tagged BACKGROUND is a profile page or a re-told old deal (the text, the url or the holding date gives it away) and sits below the bar. Pursue, Watch, Dismiss and notes are saved and shared with anyone who can open this page.

Portcos are the firm's current portfolio companies that matter to SSG: focus-area companies first, APAC-weighted, and within that the longest held at the top, because four or more years is where a sponsor starts planning the exit. The hold clock on each card fills to seven years and turns hot past four, the exit window; a story about a portco in the window scores extra Heat. A reported sale or IPO process is tagged on the card. Tap a card for the business in a sentence and its five latest headlines, which are the hand-kept signals plus a daily news search on the company's own name. Valuations are estimates with their basis beside them.

Targets are Jobi's candidates, not anything Warburg Pincus has said: companies in a focus area that look like what the firm buys, with the signals that make the case. A live process is reported by the press; everything else is a watch. Priority puts sponsor-owned companies first, longest hold at the top: a fund four or more years in is the seller most likely to run a process.
